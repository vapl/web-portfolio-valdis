  <IntroCard
    eyebrow="Case study"
    title="A shared workflow for production specifications"
    invert={true}
    maxWidth="xl"
  >
    I built an internal Next.js system for designers and design engineers at a
    manufacturing company. It brings specifications, drawings, statuses and
    project versions into one shared workflow.

    Started during my software engineering internship, the project continued
    after the internship ended. It is now in use, with further development guided
    by production needs and feedback from the team.

    The first usable prototype was ready within five days, working in sessions
    rather than full-time.
  </IntroCard>
</Section>

<Section id="challenge">
  <IntroCard
    eyebrow="The challenge"
    title="Understanding the process and connecting its data"
    invert={true}
    maxWidth="xl"
  >
    Production specifications connect product information, materials, components
    and manufacturing requirements. I needed to understand the team&apos;s process
    and model those relationships in a form people could use every day.

    Obtaining and linking the required data took most of the initial development
    effort. The challenge was making that information consistent and usable
    across the workflow.
  </IntroCard>
</Section>

<Section id="problem" title="Problem">
  <ul className="list-disc space-y-3 pl-6 text-lg text-text/80">
    <li>Specifications were stored in individual designers&apos; local folders.</li>
    <li>The team had no shared view of the order flow.</li>
    <li>Project versions lacked a controlled management process.</li>
    <li>Finding current information depended on separate files and individual knowledge.</li>
  </ul>
  <PullQuote>
    The aim was a shared production workflow that people could use and maintain
    every day.
  </PullQuote>
</Section>

<Section id="solution">
  <IntroCard
    eyebrow="Solution"
    title="From a usable prototype to a working internal tool"
    invert={true}
    maxWidth="xl"
  >
    The first prototype supported complete specification creation, drawing image
    attachments and status changes.

    The system now also includes controlled project versions and visibility over
    order flow. Requirements came from real production work, and features were
    tested with users as the system evolved.
